Rui Pan 潘睿

Hi! I am a Ph.D. student in Management & Organizations (Macro) at the Kellogg School of Management, Northwestern University, working with Prof. Dashun Wang in the Center for Science of Science and Innovation. Before starting my Ph.D., I was an M.A. student in Computational Social Science at the University of Chicago, working with Prof. James Evans in the Knowledge Lab.

My research examines how knowledge, social structure and culture shape the processes and outcomes of innovation and creativity. Methodologically, I use large-scale computational analysis of texts and social networks, and apply large language models for simulation and unstructured data collection.

Research interests

Creativity & Innovation · Knowledge · Culture · Group Dynamics · Social Networks · Computational Social Science

Research

01

Perspectives & innovation

Subjective Perspectives within Learned Representations Predict High-Impact Innovation

With Likun Cao and James Evans

Reject and Resubmit at Nature

Perspective diversity among collaborators predicts creative achievement across scientists, inventors, writers, and entrepreneurs.

Research overview

We use machine-learned language representations to quantify how collaborators see their focal work, and study how perspective diversity promotes knowledge integration. Agent-based simulations explore collaborations across combinations of knowledge and expertise.

Read the paper on arXiv
02

Culture & knowledge

Cultural Bridges in Historical Knowledge Networks

With Joshua Conrad Jackson, Seokkyun Joshua Woo, and Dashun Wang

Manuscript in preparation

The erosion of shared linguistic infrastructure fragmented scholarly networks and localized the knowledge they produced.

Research overview

Using a network of 120,828 historically notable academics and artists (1000–1899 CE), we trace the localization of elite academic networks from the 16th through 19th century to the decline of Latin as a scholarly lingua franca.

03

Social networks

Multiplex Networks in Historical Notable Creatives

With Siddharth Patwardhan and Dashun Wang

Data analysis

Influence, rivalry, collaboration, and kinship organize intellectual life in distinct ways.

Research overview

An LLM-assisted pipeline identifies relationship types in biographical data to construct a multiplex network of historically notable academics and artists. We examine the geometry and correlations of these layers across science and the arts.

04

Status & team dynamics

The Price of Recognition: How Status Inequality and Hierarchical Stability Impact Creative Team Breakups

With Mario D. Molina and Minsu Park

Data analysis completed; experiment in preparation

The instability of status hierarchies helps explain why creative teams fall apart.

Research overview

Studying K-pop bands as a setting for long-term team dynamics, we find that status disparities increase the likelihood of disbanding, especially when members alternate in their relative positions. An online group experiment is being designed to test these mechanisms causally.
